Sub-pixel jitter and motion vector reconstruction

How camera offsets reveal fine spatial detail and how depth reprojection tracks pixels across frames

dlss_motion.fsh & Halton(2,3)
Temporal sampling
Sub-pixel camera jitter
What jitter is Halton(2, 3) sequence
A tiny sub-pixel offset applied to the projection matrix each frame, shifting the camera view within [-0.5, +0.5] render pixels across a sequence of ceil(8 * ratio^2) phases.
Why jitter rebuilds detail Temporal accumulation
A static grid repeatedly samples the exact same pixel centers; shifting the camera slightly each frame lets the upscaler gather new sub-pixel samples across time to reconstruct razor-sharp edges.
Phase counts: DLAA = 8, Quality = 18, Balanced = 24, Performance = 32, Ultra Perf = 72
Velocity calculation
Motion vectors via depth reprojection
Vector convention V = Position_prev - Position_curr
Stored in render-pixel units in an RG16F texture. A fullscreen post-process shader (dlss_motion.fsh) reconstructs 3D world positions from hardware depth and reprojects them into the previous frame.
Camera translation delta Camera-relative rendering
Because Minecraft renders relative to camera position, the world origin shifts every frame. Reflexium adds back camera translation delta to prevent catastrophic smearing when walking or falling.
mat4 CurrentToPrevious = previousUnjittered * cameraDelta * inverse(currentJittered);
Special geometry handling
Why the first-person hand gets zero motion
The hand dilemma Independent animations
The player hand moves with view orientation but has independent view-bobbing and tool swing motions. Reprojecting the hand with world camera matrices creates severe tearing and shearing artifacts.
1. Depth capture
captureWorldDepth copies world depth before vanilla clears it for the hand pass.
2. Velocity zeroing
dlss_motion.fsh checks HandDepthSampler and outputs vec4(0.0) on hand pixels.
Why zero motion works: keeping hand velocity at zero prevents reprojection tearing during camera turns, keeping the weapon model stable on screen.
Future roadmap
Moving mobs and per-entity motion vectors
Current limitation Static terrain assumption
Depth reprojection assumes all pixels belong to stationary terrain. When zombies, animals, or other players walk or swing limbs, their movement does not match world matrices, creating ghost trails.
Planned solution Multiple Render Targets (MRT)
Bind the motion buffer as Color Attachment 1 during entity forward passes (entityCutout, entitySolid), writing per-vertex clip delta (Pos_prev_clip - Pos_curr_clip) to eliminate mob ghosting completely.
Planned: layout(location = 1) out vec4 outMotion; // Exact entity vertex velocity
Temporal continuity: Reflexium automatically flags reset = 1 to clear temporal history during cuts (teleports > 8 blocks, turns > 15 degrees, or frametime jumps > 250 ms).
